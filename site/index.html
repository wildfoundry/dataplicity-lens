What it shows

The system information we reach for during support work.

The commands use the same process and service identities, so related information stays connected.

01

Processes and resource use

CPU, memory, I/O, process state, parents, users and service context.

02

Services and logs

Service state, restarts and recent messages from systemd, launchd and local logs.

03

Storage and networking

Mount usage, interfaces, routes and listening sockets, linked back to local processes where possible.

04

Why we built Lens

At WildFoundry, we use Linux every day to build and support Dataplicity. Lens began as a tool for our own team: a quicker, more consistent way to inspect processes, services, logs, storage and networking while working on real systems.

Lens is not a new service or a commercial add-on. Dataplicity remains our core product, and Lens works independently of it. We are publishing Lens because it is already useful to us, and making it available may also save time for Dataplicity customers and anyone else looking after Linux or macOS systems. That is the whole idea.
